Problem 1

Consider the matrix \(A = \begin{pmatrix} 1+i & 1+i\\ -1-i & 1+i\end{pmatrix}\). Verify that \(A\) is a normal matrix. (5 points)

Solution. Recall that \(A\) is normal if \(AA^* = A^*A\), where \(A^*\) is the adjoint of \(A\), obtained by conjugating the entries of \(A\) and then transposing. Here

\[A^* = \begin{pmatrix} 1-i & -1+i\\ 1-i & 1-i\end{pmatrix}.\]

Using \((1+i)(1-i) = 2\), we compute

\[\begin{aligned} AA^* &= \begin{pmatrix} 1+i & 1+i\\ -1-i & 1+i\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1-i & -1+i\\ 1-i & 1-i\end{pmatrix}\\[4pt] &= \begin{pmatrix} (1+i)(1-i) + (1+i)(1-i) & (1+i)(-1+i) + (1+i)(1-i)\\ (-1-i)(1-i) + (1+i)(1-i) & (-1-i)(-1+i) + (1+i)(1-i)\end{pmatrix}\\[4pt] &= \begin{pmatrix} 2 + 2 & -2 + 2\\ -2 + 2 & 2 + 2\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 0\\ 0 & 4\end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}\]

and

\[\begin{aligned} A^*A &= \begin{pmatrix} 1-i & -1+i\\ 1-i & 1-i\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1+i & 1+i\\ -1-i & 1+i\end{pmatrix}\\[4pt] &= \begin{pmatrix} (1-i)(1+i) + (-1+i)(-1-i) & (1-i)(1+i) + (-1+i)(1+i)\\ (1-i)(1+i) + (1-i)(-1-i) & (1-i)(1+i) + (1-i)(1+i)\end{pmatrix}\\[4pt] &= \begin{pmatrix} 2 + 2 & 2 - 2\\ 2 - 2 & 2 + 2\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 0\\ 0 & 4\end{pmatrix}. \end{aligned}\]

Thus \(AA^* = 4I_2 = A^*A\), so \(A\) is normal.

Problem 2

Find a complex orthogonal matrix \(Q\) diagonalizing \(A\). You do not have to check that \(Q\) diagonalizes \(A\). (5 points)

Solution. Eigenvalues. Using \((1+i)^2 = 1 + 2i + i^2 = 2i\),

\[\begin{aligned} p_A(x) = |xI_2 - A| &= \begin{vmatrix} x - (1+i) & -(1+i)\\ 1+i & x - (1+i)\end{vmatrix}\\[4pt] &= \big(x - (1+i)\big)^2 - \big(-(1+i)\big)(1+i)\\[4pt] &= x^2 - 2(1+i)x + (1+i)^2 + (1+i)^2\\[4pt] &= x^2 - (2 + 2i)x + 4i = (x - 2)(x - 2i). \end{aligned}\]

The eigenvalues of \(A\) are \(\lambda = 2\) and \(\lambda = 2i\).

Eigenvectors. For \(\lambda = 2\):

\[A - 2I = \begin{pmatrix} -1+i & 1+i\\ -1-i & -1+i\end{pmatrix}.\]

The first row gives \((-1+i)x + (1+i)y = 0\), so \(y = \frac{1-i}{1+i}\,x = \frac{(1-i)^2}{2}\,x = -ix\). Taking \(x = 1\), \(E_2\) is spanned by \(v_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1\\ -i\end{pmatrix}\). (Check with the second row: \((-1-i)(1) + (-1+i)(-i) = (-1 - i) + (i + 1) = 0\).)

For \(\lambda = 2i\):

\[A - 2iI = \begin{pmatrix} 1-i & 1+i\\ -1-i & 1-i\end{pmatrix}.\]

The first row gives \((1-i)x + (1+i)y = 0\), so \(y = -\frac{1-i}{1+i}\,x = ix\). Taking \(x = 1\), \(E_{2i}\) is spanned by \(v_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1\\ i\end{pmatrix}\).

Orthonormalize. With respect to the complex inner product \(v\cdot w = a_1\overline{b_1} + a_2\overline{b_2}\) on \(\mathbb{C}^2\), where \(v = \begin{pmatrix} a_1\\ a_2\end{pmatrix}\) and \(w = \begin{pmatrix} b_1\\ b_2\end{pmatrix}\),

\[v_1\cdot v_2 = 1\,\overline{1} + (-i)\,\overline{i} = 1 + (-i)(-i) = 1 + i^2 = 0,\]

so \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) are orthogonal, as they must be, since \(A\) is normal and the eigenvalues are distinct. Also \(\|v_1\|^2 = v_1\cdot v_1 = 1 + (-i)(i) = 2\) and \(\|v_2\|^2 = v_2\cdot v_2 = 1 + (i)(-i) = 2\), so normalizing gives

\[u_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix} 1\\ -i\end{pmatrix}, \qquad u_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix} 1\\ i\end{pmatrix}.\]

Therefore

\[Q = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1\\ -i & i\end{pmatrix}\]

is a complex orthogonal matrix, i.e., \(Q^{-1} = Q^*\), and

\[Q^{-1}AQ = Q^*AQ = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0\\ 0 & 2i\end{pmatrix}.\]
