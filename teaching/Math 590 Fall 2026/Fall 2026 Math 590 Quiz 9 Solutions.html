Fall 2026: Math 590 Quiz 9 Solutions

Problem 1

Let \(V\) be a vector space over \(\mathbb{R}\) and \(\langle\ ,\ \rangle\) a function from \(V\times V \to \mathbb{R}\). State two of the four properties required in order that \(\langle\ ,\ \rangle\) be an inner product on \(V\). (4 points)

Solution. An inner product on \(V\) is a function \(\langle\ ,\ \rangle : V\times V \to \mathbb{R}\) satisfying the following four properties, for all \(u, v, w \in V\) and \(c \in \mathbb{R}\). Any two of them answer the question.

  1. (i) \(\langle u + v, w\rangle = \langle u, w\rangle + \langle v, w\rangle\).
  2. (ii) \(\langle cu, w\rangle = c\langle u, w\rangle\).
  3. (iii) \(\langle v, w\rangle = \langle w, v\rangle\).
  4. (iv) \(\langle v, v\rangle \geq 0\), and \(\langle v, v\rangle = 0\) if and only if \(v = 0\).

Problem 2

For the vectors \(w_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1\\0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}\), \(w_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0\\2\\2\\0\end{pmatrix}\), \(v_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1\\0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}\), note first that \(w_1, w_2\) are orthogonal. Find a third vector, say \(w_3\), so that \(\mathrm{Span}\{w_1, w_2, v_3\} = \mathrm{Span}\{w_1, w_2, w_3\}\) and \(\{w_1, w_2, w_3\}\) is an orthogonal set. (6 points)

Solution. First, \(w_1 \cdot w_2 = 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 = 0\), so \(w_1\) and \(w_2\) are orthogonal. Following the Gram-Schmidt process, we take

\[w_3 = v_3 - \frac{v_3 \cdot w_1}{w_1 \cdot w_1}\, w_1 - \frac{v_3 \cdot w_2}{w_2 \cdot w_2}\, w_2.\]

Here \(v_3 \cdot w_1 = 1\), \(w_1 \cdot w_1 = 2\), \(v_3 \cdot w_2 = 2\) and \(w_2 \cdot w_2 = 8\), so

\[w_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1\\0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix} - \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1\\0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix} - \frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix} 0\\2\\2\\0\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2}\\[2pt] -\frac{1}{2}\\[2pt] \frac{1}{2}\\[2pt] -\frac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix}.\]

Check: \(w_3 \cdot w_1 = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2} = 0\) and \(w_3 \cdot w_2 = -1 + 1 = 0\), so \(w_1, w_2, w_3\) are mutually orthogonal.

(Any nonzero multiple of \(w_3\), such as \(\begin{pmatrix} 1\\-1\\1\\-1\end{pmatrix}\), works equally well.)
