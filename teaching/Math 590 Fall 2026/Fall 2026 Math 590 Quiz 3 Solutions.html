Problem 1

Let \(V = \mathrm{Span}\{v_1, \ldots, v_r\}\) and \(w_1, \ldots, w_s\in V\) be linearly independent. What conclusions can you draw from the Exchange Theorem? (4 points)

Solution. Since \(w_1, \ldots, w_s\) is a linearly independent subset of \(V = \mathrm{Span}\{v_1, \ldots, v_r\}\), the Exchange Theorem allows us to conclude:

  1. (a) \(s\le r\), and
  2. (b) after a suitable reordering of \(v_1, \ldots, v_r\), the set
    \[\{w_1, \ldots, w_s,\ v_{s+1}, \ldots, v_r\}\]
    spans \(V\).

Problem 2

Extend \(\begin{pmatrix} 1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix} -1\\1\\-1\end{pmatrix}\) to a basis for \(\mathbb{R}^3\). You must justify your answer. (3 points)

Solution. Let \(v_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}\) and \(v_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -1\\1\\-1\end{pmatrix}\). Since \(\mathbb{R}^3\) has dimension \(3\), we need one more vector, not in \(\mathrm{Span}\{v_1, v_2\}\), to extend \(\{v_1, v_2\}\) to a basis.

Try \(v_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}\). Suppose \(v_3 = av_1+bv_2\) for some \(a, b\in\mathbb{R}\). Comparing entries gives

\[a-b = 1\ (\text{1st entry}), \qquad b = 0\ (\text{2nd entry}), \qquad a-b = 0\ (\text{3rd entry}).\]

The 1st and 3rd equations require \(a-b\) to equal both \(1\) and \(0\) simultaneously, a contradiction. So \(v_3\notin\mathrm{Span}\{v_1, v_2\}\), and \(\{v_1, v_2, v_3\}\) is linearly independent.

As a check, we verify directly that \(\{v_1, v_2, v_3\}\) is linearly independent by computing the determinant of the matrix with these vectors as columns:

\[\det\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 1\\0 & 1 & 0\\1 & -1 & 0\end{pmatrix}.\]

Expanding along the second row (which has only one nonzero entry):

\[\det = (-1)^{2+2}(1)\det\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1\\1 & 0\end{pmatrix} = 1\cdot(0-1) = -1\ne 0.\]

Since the determinant is nonzero, \(\{v_1, v_2, v_3\}\) is linearly independent. As \(3\) linearly independent vectors in the \(3\)-dimensional space \(\mathbb{R}^3\), they automatically form a basis. Hence

\[\left\{\begin{pmatrix} 1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix},\ \begin{pmatrix} -1\\1\\-1\end{pmatrix},\ \begin{pmatrix} 1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}\right\}\]

is a basis for \(\mathbb{R}^3\) extending the given vectors.

Problem 3

Let \(T: V\to W\) be a linear transformation between vector spaces over \(F\). Let \(K\) be the set of vectors \(w\in V\) such that \(T(w) = \vec{0}\). Show that \(K\) is closed under addition and scalar multiplication. (3 points)

Solution. Closed under addition: Let \(v_1, v_2\in K\), so \(T(v_1) = \vec{0}\) and \(T(v_2) = \vec{0}\). Since \(T\) is linear,

\[T(v_1+v_2) = T(v_1)+T(v_2) = \vec{0}+\vec{0} = \vec{0}.\]

Hence \(v_1+v_2\in K\).

Closed under scalar multiplication: Let \(v\in K\) and let \(c\in F\) be a scalar. Since \(T\) is linear,

\[T(cv) = c\,T(v) = c\cdot\vec{0} = \vec{0}.\]

Hence \(cv\in K\).

Therefore \(K\) is closed under both addition and scalar multiplication.
